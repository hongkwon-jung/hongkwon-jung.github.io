Etching 100 nm SiO2 on a New Tool

Measurement and Process Parameters

  • Defined the etch rate from the clear time of 100 nm thermal oxide, checked in 10 s steps, and confirmed it with one continuous run
  • Fixed power and frequency at the source limits first, then tuned the gas composition, then the pressure

03Troubleshooting

Rate-Limit Diagnosis

Power cannot isolate the limit

Situation
Single power source for plasma generation and ion acceleration
Diagnosis
Raising power increases ion flux, ion energy and F radicals together
Action
Fixed at the 200 W maximum, where PR damage stayed small on this low-power tool
Result
Limit tested with frequency and gas instead

Frequency test

Situation
3 – 5 nm/min at 50 kHz
Diagnosis
At 100 kHz the sheath voltage (ion energy) drops, yet the rate rose
Action
Ruled out ion energy and suspected ion supply
Result
≥ 8.3 nm/min

Gas composition test

Situation
Higher frequency also raises CF4 dissociation, so F radicals were not yet excluded
Diagnosis
Halving CF4 while doubling Ar raised the rate to ≈30 nm/min
Action
Ruled out F radicals, set Ar to 74% inside the flat 60 – 78% range
Result
≈33 nm/min after pressure tuning

CHF3 mixing

Situation
Adding CHF3 to CF4 weakened the plasma
Action
Separated the rate recipe (CF4) from the selectivity recipe (CHF3)

05Lessons

Lessons

  1. On a single-power tool one knob shifts several plasma quantities at once, so the rate limit is isolated with tests whose effects point in opposite directions
  2. The same process is optimized differently on each tool, so the rate limit on that tool is checked before transferring a literature condition
