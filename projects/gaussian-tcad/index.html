TCAD Modeling of WSe2/IGZO Gaussian Transistors

Effects of structural parameters on the Gaussian response in WSe2/IGZO heterojunction transistors

The first TCAD work in the lab. Using a Sentaurus model calibrated to measurements, this study identified the mechanism of the Gaussian response, quantified the effects of seven structural parameters, and proposed application-specific design guidelines.

01Objective

Formation Conditions and Design Guidelines of the Gaussian Response

A Gaussian (anti-ambipolar) transistor conducts current only within a specific gate-voltage range. The goal was to identify the conditions under which this response arises in a heterojunction of p-type WSe2 and n-type IGZO, quantify the effect of each structural parameter, and propose application-specific design guidelines.

Gate / dielectricp++ Si back gate / Al2O3 90 nm
ChannelsIGZO 10 nm, WSe2 5 nm (8 layers)
ElectrodesAl (IGZO side), Pd (WSe2 side), asymmetric
BiasVDS = 1 V
02Approach

Measurement-Calibrated Physical Models and Structural Parameter Analysis

As this was the first TCAD work in the lab, there were no previous materials or handover. I built the physical models from the manual and literature and calibrated them to measurements of single WSe2 and IGZO TFTs fabricated in the lab.

  • Fixed about 100 model parameters to literature values or tuned them one at a time to separate the dominant physics (traps, gate leakage, mobility) in each gate-voltage range
  • Applied SRH recombination, IGZO percolation mobility, and nonlocal tunneling models in 2D drift-diffusion calculations

Analysis of seven structural parameters

With all other conditions fixed, each structural parameter was varied one at a time to analyze changes in peak voltage (VP), peak current (IP), full width at half maximum (FWHM), and peak-to-valley ratio (PVR).

03Troubleshooting

Simulation Troubleshooting and Automation

Convergence failure

Situation
The calculation stopped at +0.3 V after the gate leakage model was added
Diagnosis
Located, from the log, the point where the electron density at the contact edge collapsed to zero
Action
Modified only the mesh at that corner
Result
Ran to completion up to +10 V

Simulation time

Situation
One run took 13 hours
Diagnosis
Found from the log that matrix assembly took 43 times longer than solving
Action
Adjusted the iteration settings
Result
Reduced to 6 hours

Mechanism verification

Situation
Needed to determine whether the Gaussian peak current comes from SRH recombination or from carrier overlap
Diagnosis
A 100-fold change in SRH lifetime changed the peak current by less than 0.03%
Action
Interpreted the recombination distribution as an indicator of the carrier-overlap region, and checked that the conclusion holds when the interface trap density is varied
Result
Published with the interpretation that simultaneous n–p conduction and WSe2-side carrier overlap determine the response

Fitting automation

Situation
Each model redesign required about two months of repeated manual fitting
Action
Built an AI pipeline with six roles: evidence research, sensitivity calculation, next-condition design, execution, evaluation, and an audit that rejects fits that only match the numbers. I decided the physical models and the priority characteristics for each voltage range myself.
Result
31 of 39 IGZO fitting runs converged
04Results

Response Mechanism and Structural Design Guidelines

Mechanism governing the response

Analysis with band diagrams, electron/hole current decomposition, and SRH recombination distributions showed that the peak current is determined not by the turn-on of a single channel or the simple sum of two currents, but by the simultaneous conduction of the n- and p-channels and carrier overlap on the WSe2 side.

  • Changing the WSe2 SRH lifetime 100-fold changed the integrated recombination proportionally, but the peak current by less than 0.03%
  • The recombination distribution is therefore interpreted not as a current contributor but as a spatial and bias indicator of the n–p carrier-overlap region

Response change by structural parameter

  • A clear peak appears with Al on the IGZO side and Pd on the WSe2 side, and it is strongly suppressed when the placement is reversed. With symmetric Al/Al or Pd/Pd contacts, one carrier supply weakens and the peak is reduced
  • With the same metal on both contacts, a work function of 4.7 eV balances the IGZO electron and WSe2 hole supplies and gives the most pronounced peak, which decreases at 4.5 and 4.9 eV
  • Peak formation is not governed by the effective channel length. Shortening the WSe2 effective length from 25 to 1 µm increases the right-valley current by about two orders of magnitude and IP by about 6×

Structural design guidelines

MetricReference structureMonolayer WSe2Change
Average PVR4.06×1063.66×107≈9.0×
FWHM1.042 V0.633 V−39.3%

  • Contact work function is the top design parameter, with the largest effect on IP, VP, average PVR, and both rising and falling SS, while FWHM is determined by Vth alignment
  • Monolayer WSe2 favors selectivity and multilayer WSe2 favors output current, so the layer number is a trade-off to be chosen by application
  • A single metal with an intermediate work function, used for both contacts, can replace the asymmetric Al/Pd contacts to simplify the process
05Lessons

Lessons

  1. With many model parameters, separating those to fix from those to tune is what makes it possible to distinguish the physics governing measured characteristics from the effects of structural parameters
  2. Even with a new tool learned without reference materials, once its principles are established, the repetitive work can be automated to shorten the verification cycle
